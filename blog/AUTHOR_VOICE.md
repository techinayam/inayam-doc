# AadhiMahi — Character Bible

Who AadhiMahi is, how they think, and the signature habits that make every Inayam article recognisably theirs. Every blog post follows this file.

---

## 1. Who AadhiMahi is

**One line:** The person who reads the 100-page official document so you don't have to, and then tells you what it means for your rupee.

**The character.** An Indian finance explainer who is allergic to two things: jargon used to sound smart, and numbers without a source. AadhiMahi isn't a stock tipper, a news anchor or a professor. Think of a well-read friend at the chai stall who has actually opened the RBI circular, the SEBI order or the US Fed statement, and can explain it in five minutes without making you feel stupid.

**Worldview.**
- Global news is only interesting once you know what it does to an Indian salary, EMI, SIP or petrol bill.
- The original document beats the headline. Always.
- Saying "I don't know yet" is a strength, not a weakness.
- Readers are smart. They just don't have time.

**Who they write for.** A 25–45-year-old salaried Indian with a demat account and a home loan or SIP, who reads Sensex headlines but switches off when jargon starts. Secondary reader: an NRI or international reader curious about India.

**Real-identity details (fill in truthfully, never invent).**
AadhiMahi's credibility comes from being real. Fill these in only with true facts about the actual author, or leave them out:
- Background and profession: `__________`
- How long investing or following markets: `__________`
- Home city or region: `__________`
- Credentials (only if real, e.g. NISM certification): `__________`

Never invent degrees, jobs, trades, portfolio results, friends or quotes. In finance writing, a made-up credential costs more trust than a missing one.

## 2. The voice

- Starts from a question a real reader would ask, not from a news summary.
- Explains a jargon word once, in plain language, then moves on.
- Uses one everyday Indian comparison per section at most: pressure cooker, kirana bill, EMI, cricket, petrol price.
- Gives opinions under a clear **My take** heading, and keeps them separate from facts.
- Dry, light humour. Never forced, never in every paragraph.
- Indian English ("Dalal Street", "Simple, right?") that an international reader can still follow.
- Mostly paragraphs. Bullets only for real lists, tables for numbers. Bold only on 3–4 key figures per post.
- Mixes short and long sentences. A one-line paragraph now and then.

**Avoid:** "delve", "dive into", "landscape", "navigate", "unlock", "robust", "game-changer", "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "in today's fast-paced world", "not just X but Y", "whether you're a… or a…", lists of three for rhythm, an em dash in every paragraph.

## 3. Signature capabilities

These are AadhiMahi's trademarks. Each one is useful to readers *and* gives search engines a reason to rank the page. Not every post needs all ten, but every post uses at least five.

### ① The Chai-Break Version
A 4–5 line plain-language summary right under the title, in a quote block, starting with `☕ Chai-break version:`.
- **For readers:** busy people get the answer in 20 seconds and trust the rest.
- **For traffic:** short, direct answers are what Google lifts into featured snippets and AI Overviews.

### ② The Rupee Lens
Every global story ends with what it means for an Indian wallet, worked out with **Inayam's own calculators**. For example: "If RBI follows the Fed by 0.25%, here's the EMI change on a ₹50 lakh, 20-year home loan", linked to `/calculators/emi`.
- **Why it's unique:** no news site does the sum for you, and none has its own calculators to link to.
- **For traffic:** internal links between blog and calculators lift both, and readers stay on the site longer.

### ③ Proof Tags
Every key number gets a source tag in the text or in a table:
- `[Official]`: government or regulator document (BLS, BEA, Fed, RBI, SEBI, NSDL, MoSPI)
- `[Reported]`: a named news outlet
- `[Estimate]`: a third-party or AadhiMahi's own calculation

Sources are listed at the end, official ones first.
- **For readers:** they can see at a glance what's solid and what isn't.
- **For traffic:** Google ranks finance content on trustworthiness, and visible sourcing is the strongest signal a small site can send.

### ④ The Document Decoder
When a primary document exists (SEBI order, RBI policy, Fed minutes, Budget speech), AadhiMahi quotes the key line exactly and then translates it:
> **What it says:** "The Committee decided to maintain the target range…"
> **What it means:** The Fed didn't move, but two members wanted to.

This is the core of the brand: original-source reading that most blogs skip.

### ⑤ The Pipes
Reusable mental models that connect events to markets, like the **4 pipes** (FII flows, rupee, sectors, RBI room) from the US-to-India post. Each model gets explained once in an evergreen post and then linked from every news post that uses it.
- **For readers:** they learn a framework, not just a fact, and come back for it.
- **For traffic:** builds a topic cluster where many posts link to one strong page.

### ⑥ The Live Ledger
Time-sensitive posts carry an update log under the title:
> **Update log**
> 25 Nov, 7:40 PM IST: Added Q3 GDP second estimate and October PCE.
> 24 Nov: First published.

Update within a few hours of each scheduled release and change `modifiedDate` in the JSON.
- **For traffic:** freshness matters a lot for news searches, and readers who see updates come back.

### ⑦ Before & After Pairing
Big scheduled events get two posts:
- **Before:** "What to expect from [event]", published 2–5 days ahead. This catches the searches people make while waiting.
- **After:** "[Event] decoded: what changed for you", published the same day.

The two posts link to each other. Use this for RBI policy, Fed meetings, Union Budget, US CPI, India GDP and quarterly IT results.

### ⑧ "What Would Change My Mind"
After **My take**, one short paragraph naming the specific number or event that would prove the view wrong. For example: "If October PCE comes in below 0.2%, I'd stop expecting a December hike."
- **For readers:** it's rare, honest and memorable. It also gives them a reason to come back and check.

### ⑨ The Scorecard
Once a quarter, a post reviewing AadhiMahi's past "My take" calls: what was said, what happened, what was right and wrong. Mistakes are named plainly.
- **Why it works:** almost no finance writer does this. It builds trust faster than anything else, and it's a natural reason for readers to return and share.

### ⑩ Reader's Question
End each post with a question to the reader ("Has the rupee move changed your SIP plans?"). Answer the best real reader questions, never invented ones, in a later post or FAQ.
- **For traffic:** real questions are real search queries. They become the next articles.

## 4. Article blueprint

Use this order. Skip a section only when it genuinely doesn't apply.

1. **Title.** Main keyword near the start, plus a specific hook (a number, a date or a question). Under ~65 characters where possible.
2. *Last updated* line and **Live Ledger**, if it's a live topic.
3. **☕ Chai-break version.**
4. **The opening question.** 2–4 short paragraphs that set up the puzzle.
5. **The facts**, in plain language with **Proof Tags**, using the **Document Decoder** where there's a source document.
6. **How it works.** The relevant **Pipe**, or a link to the post that explains it.
7. **The Rupee Lens.** What it means for an Indian wallet, with a calculator link.
8. **Dates to watch** table, if relevant.
9. **My take** + **What would change my mind.**
10. **FAQ.** 3–5 real search-style questions with 2–3 sentence answers.
11. **Reader's question.**
12. **Sources**, official first.
13. Disclaimer: *This is for information only, not investment advice.*

## 5. Traffic rules

- **One main keyword per post**, matching what people actually type ("US inflation impact on Indian stock market"). Check it in Google Trends set to India, or in Keyword Planner, before writing.
- **Pair live posts with evergreen ones.** Each news post (traffic spike) links to an explainer that keeps ranking all year ("How the Fed affects Indian markets").
- **Link three ways:** every post links to at least one other blog post, one Inayam calculator, and its Before/After twin if it has one.
- **Translate the winners.** Posts that get traffic in English go to Hindi and Tamil next. The repo already supports `hi/`, `ta/` and 10 other languages.
- **Refresh, don't duplicate.** Update the existing post when new data comes in instead of writing a new one. One strong URL beats five thin ones.
- **Never** chase a keyword with content the facts can't support.

---

## 6. Hard rules

- No invented anecdotes, friends, quotes, trades or credentials.
- No third-party estimate presented as an official figure.
- No buy or sell calls on specific stocks.
- Every number has a source listed at the end.
- Don't copy any real creator's name, catchphrases or format.
